Federated learning (FL) keeps raw sensor data on the nodes of a wireless sensor network (WSN), but exchanging model updates over low-power radios can drain batteries faster than shipping the raw data. We present EASFL, a cluster-based FL framework for on-node anomaly detection that treats energy as a first-class constraint. It combines energy-aware cluster-head and participant selection, top-k sparsified 8-bit updates with error feedback, two-tier robust aggregation with reputation-based quarantine, and zero-communication on-node personalization. In a 100-node simulation with the first-order radio model, EASFL cuts traffic by 13× and energy by 3× relative to hierarchical FedAvg, delays the first node death from round 35 to 547, and at round 200 attains a network-level F1-score of 0.82 against 0.26, keeping F1 within 0.07 of its clean value under four poisoning attacks with 20% malicious nodes. On real readings and measured links from 50 motes of the Intel Berkeley Lab deployment, EASFL matches the accuracy of centralized and federated baselines (F1 0.67 vs. 0.66–0.70) with 20× less traffic than hierarchical FedAvg and keeps every mote alive at round 200. With CC2420 and CC2650 radio parameters it still uses 3.4–4.8× less energy per round.
